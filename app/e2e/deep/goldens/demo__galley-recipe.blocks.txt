<h1 dir="auto" id="moqueca-for-twelve">Moqueca for twelve</h1>
--- p 11-11
<p dir="auto">A Bahian fish stew of coconut milk, tomatoes and red palm oil, cooked in one big pot on the <em>Petrel</em>'s two-burner stove. It feeds the whole crew, forgives a rolling sea, and tastes like home.</p>

--- h2 17-17
<h2 dir="auto" id="why-this-at-sea">Why this, at sea</h2>
--- p 19-19
<p dir="auto">On the <em>Petrel</em>, dinner is the one hour when nobody watches a sensor. A moqueca suits a small galley: one pot, no oven, nothing to fry, and nobody minds if it waits half an hour for the wind to drop.</p>
--- p 21-21
<p dir="auto">I learned it from my grandmother, who never measured anything. These amounts are my best guess at her handfuls, tested on twelve hungry people during the October voyage (<a href="field-journal.md">Nine Days on the Petrel</a>).</p>

<p dir="auto">You also need a big bunch of cilantro for every four people, one fresh malagueta or other hot chili, a splash of olive oil, and salt.</p>

<h2 dir="auto" id="scaling">Scaling</h2>
--- p 40-40
<p dir="auto">Every amount in the table follows one rule, <span data-math-style="inline">q_{12} = 3\,q_4</span>, with one exception: the salt<sup class="footnote-ref"><a data-footnote-ref="" href="#fn-salt" id="fnref-salt">2</a></sup>.</p>
--- h2 42-42
<h2 dir="auto" id="method">Method</h2>
--- ol 44-59
<ol>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Marinate the fish.</strong>
<ol>
<li dir="auto">Cut it into thick steaks or chunks about 5 cm across.</li>
<li dir="auto">Rub with the lime juice, crushed garlic and a little salt.</li>
<li dir="auto">Leave it in the cool box for 20 to 30 minutes while you slice everything else.</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Slice the vegetables.</strong>
<ol>
<li dir="auto">Onions and peppers in thin rings, tomatoes in rounds.</li>
<li dir="auto">Chop the cilantro, stems and all, and keep a handful of leaves for the end.</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Build the layers</strong> in a wide, heavy pot, off the heat.
<ol>
<li dir="auto">A splash of olive oil, then half the onions, tomatoes and peppers.</li>
<li dir="auto">The fish in a single layer, with its marinade.</li>
<li dir="auto">The rest of the vegetables, half the cilantro and the chili.</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Pour in the coconut milk</strong> and drizzle the dendê over the top.</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Cook covered</strong> over medium heat for 20 to 25 minutes, until the fish flakes.
<ol>
<li dir="auto">Do not stir. Shake the pot gently now and then so nothing sticks.</li>
<li dir="auto">At sea, clamp the pot in the stove's fiddle rails before you light the burner.</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Finish.</strong> Taste the broth, adjust the salt and lime, scatter the last of the cilantro, and let it rest off the heat for 5 minutes.</li>
</ol>
--- p 61-61
<p dir="auto">Serve with white rice and farofa, cassava flour toasted in butter.</p>
--- div 63-65
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note">
<p class="markdown-alert-title" dir="auto">Chef's note</p>
<p dir="auto">A moqueca is never stirred. The layers cook in their own steam, so the fish stays in whole pieces. If the <em>Petrel</em> rolls hard, take the pot off the heat and wait it out: a moqueca that rests is a better moqueca.</p>
</div>
--- h2 67-67
<h2 dir="auto" id="swaps">Swaps</h2>
--- details 72-77
<details>
<summary>Swaps for a short-stocked galley</summary>
<ul>
<li dir="auto"><strong>No dendê:</strong> olive oil with a teaspoon of sweet paprika gives the color, though not the flavor.</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>Shrimp instead of fish:</strong> add peeled shrimp for the last 5 minutes only, or they turn rubbery.</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>No fish at all:</strong> ripe plantain and hearts of palm make a lovely <em>moqueca de banana-da-terra</em>.</li>
<li dir="auto"><strong>No cilantro:</strong> parsley and scallions, the <em>cheiro-verde</em> of much of Brazil.</li>
<li dir="auto"><del>Coconut cream</del> <strong>Coconut milk:</strong> our first version used cream, and on a warm day at sea it was far too heavy.</li>
</ul>
</details>

<li dir="auto" id="fn-dende">
<p dir="auto">Dendê is unrefined oil from the fruit of the African oil palm. It came to Brazil with enslaved West Africans and is at the heart of Afro-Bahian cooking. It gives moqueca its color and its deep, slightly nutty flavor. Look for oil from certified sustainable sources. <a aria-label="Back to reference 1" class="footnote-backref" data-footnote-backref-idx="1" data-footnote-backref="" href="#fnref-dende">↩</a></p>
</li>
<li dir="auto" id="fn-salt">
<p dir="auto">Salt is seasoned to taste, not by arithmetic. A pot of the same shape with three times the volume has only about twice the surface (<span data-math-style="inline">3^{2/3} \approx 2.08</span>), so it loses relatively less liquid as it simmers, and the salt in the marinade varies with the fish. Start with twice the salt, taste near the end, then add. <a aria-label="Back to reference 2" class="footnote-backref" data-footnote-backref-idx="2" data-footnote-backref="" href="#fnref-salt">↩</a></p>
</li>
</ol>
