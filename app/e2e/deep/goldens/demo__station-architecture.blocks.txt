<h1 id="how-a-reading-travels">How a reading travels</h1>
--- p 11-11
<p>Every ten minutes, twelve buoys on Larkhaven Sound wake up, measure the water and go back to sleep. This page follows one reading from a sensor one meter down to your screen, a trip of less than a minute.</p>

<p>From the API, the same numbers reach the harbor dashboard, Gull's weekly bulletin and any researcher who asks. Nothing on this path is exotic: each part is small, boring and replaceable, which is exactly what you want from equipment that spends the winter in salt water.</p>
--- h2 30-30
<h2 id="the-40-second-wake-cycle">The 40-second wake cycle</h2>
--- p 32-32
<p>A buoy spends most of its life asleep. Its <abbr title="microcontroller unit">MCU</abbr> wakes on a timer, powers the sensors, takes a reading, sends it and goes back to sleep. One wake lasts 40 seconds; the other 9 minutes and 20 seconds are quiet.</p>
--- pre 34-59
<pre><code class="language-plantuml">@startuml
skinparam shadowing false
skinparam roundCorner 10
skinparam ArrowColor #64748b
participant "Buoy MCU" as MCU
participant "Sensors" as SEN
participant "LoRa radio" as RAD
participant "Gateway" as GW
participant "Ingest" as ING

== Wake, t = 0 s ==
MCU -&gt; SEN : power on
SEN --&gt; MCU : ready after 20 s warm-up
MCU -&gt; SEN : sample for 8 s
SEN --&gt; MCU : T, S, pH, DO + wave summary
== Send, t = 28 s ==
MCU -&gt; RAD : 24-byte frame
RAD -&gt; GW : uplink
GW --&gt; RAD : ack
GW -&gt; ING : forward frame
== Sleep, t = 40 s ==
MCU -&gt; SEN : power off
MCU -&gt; MCU : sleep 9 min 20 s
@enduml
</code></pre>
--- p 61-61
<p>The gateway answers the radio at once and forwards the frame on its own time, so the radio never waits for the shore. Its listening window stays at three seconds.</p>
--- h2 63-63
<h2 id="power-during-a-wake">Power during a wake</h2>
--- p 65-65
<p>Where does the energy go? Mostly into warming up the sensors. The radio, despite its reputation, is on for barely four seconds.</p>

--- p 112-112
<p>Add it up and one wake costs about 19 joules. With the wave sensor ticking along between wakes, a buoy uses under 1 Wh a day: the 10 W panels earn that back in a few minutes of full sun, and the 154 Wh battery could carry a buoy through five months of gray skies.</p>

<h2 id="inside-a-buoy">Inside a buoy</h2>
--- p 116-116
<p>Each buoy is a float, a short tower and a sensor cage, held in place by a chain. The electronics bay sits inside the float, just above the waterline.</p>

<h2 id="a-buoys-life">A buoy's life</h2>
--- p 164-164
<p>A buoy is always in one of a few states. Most of the season it simply alternates between sampling and sleeping.</p>

--- h2 182-182
<h2 id="the-data-model">The data model</h2>
--- p 184-184
<p>Behind the API sit five tables. A station hosts one deployment for every buoy moored there; each deployment carries its sensors and records its readings; a reading may collect QC flags.</p>

--- p 222-222
<p>Readings belong to a deployment, not to a station. A buoy swapped mid-season never mixes its numbers with its replacement's.</p>
--- h2 224-224
<h2 id="configuration">Configuration</h2>
--- p 226-226
<p>Two plain files steer the whole pipeline. The buoy reads <a href="station/buoy.toml">its settings</a> at every boot, and the ingest service checks each reading against <a href="station/qc-rules.yaml">the QC rules</a>. Here are the first lines of the rules:</p>
--- pre 228-243
<pre><code class="language-yaml" data-lines="4-6" data-title="station/qc-rules.yaml"># Tidewell QC rules, applied by the ingest service to every reading.
# Units follow each sensor: °C, PSU, pH units, mg/L and meters.
version: 3
order: [range, spike, flatline, drift]
reject_on: [range]
publish_flagged: true

# Physical limits as [min, max]. Outside them, a reading is rejected.
range:
  temp_c: [-2.0, 35.0]
  sal_psu: [0.0, 42.0]
  ph: [7.2, 8.6]
  do_mgl: [0.0, 15.0]
  hs_m: [0.0, 12.0]
</code></pre>
--- p 245-245
<p>Lines 4 to 6 are the whole policy: the order of the checks, the one failure that rejects a reading outright, and the promise that flagged readings are still published, flag attached. When you want to point a colleague at a rule, a reference like <code>station/qc-rules.yaml:5</code> opens the file at that line. The early warning about Kittiwake Shoal's pH sensor is at <code>station/ingest-2026-11-12.log:22</code>.</p>
--- h2 247-247
<h2 id="decision-lora-not-cellular">Decision: LoRa, not cellular</h2>
--- p 249-249
<p>We chose the radio before we chose anything else, because it decides the battery, the budget and where buoys can go.</p>
--- details 254-264
<details>
<summary>ADR-007 · Use LoRa with our own gateways (accepted, June 2025)</summary>
<p><strong>Context.</strong> Buoys send 24 bytes every 10 minutes from up to 14 km offshore. The inner Sound, around Heron Flats, has patchy cell coverage.</p>

<p><strong>Decision.</strong> LoRa in the US915 band, spreading factor 9, three gateways on land we can reach by car.</p>
<p><strong>Consequences.</strong> No monthly fees and no gaps, but we maintain three gateways ourselves. Frames must stay small: at spreading factor 9, a frame has to fit in the band's 400 ms limit on air.</p>
