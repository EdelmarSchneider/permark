<h1 id="how-we-write-the-larkhaven-style-guide">How we write: the Larkhaven style guide</h1>
--- p 11-11
<p>Good pages get read. This is how we keep ours short, true and kind to the reader, with a quick reference to every mark Markdown gives you.</p>
--- pre 13-29
<pre><code class="language-mermaid">mindmap

--- h2 31-31
<h2 id="write-for-one-reader">Write for one reader</h2>
--- p 33-33
<p>Before you type, name one reader and the one job they came to do. A volunteer on the pier at six in the morning needs the swap checklist, not the history of LH-09. Everything else moves lower, or goes.</p>
--- p 35-35
<p>That reader is sometimes an agent. Gull reads our pages too, so the same rules apply: say what you mean, give numbers their units and dates. Its own <a href="bulletin-agent.md">prompt file</a> shows how tags like <code>&lt;context&gt;</code> and placeholders like <code>{{week}}</code> read on the page.</p>
--- p 37-37
<p>Three questions before you publish:</p>
--- ol 39-41
<ol>
<li>Who is this page for?</li>
<li>What will they do after reading it?</li>
<li>Which number would they quote?</li>
</ol>
--- h2 43-43
<h2 id="headings-lists-and-tasks">Headings, lists and tasks</h2>
--- p 45-45
<p>One title, then sentence-case sections, one idea each. Use a list when the reader will scan or tick things off, and nest one level at most. You type:</p>
--- pre 47-54
<pre><code class="language-markdown">1. Hull
   - Inspect the hatch seal
   - Clean the solar panel

   - [x] Calibrate pH
   - [ ] Replace the oxygen membrane
</code></pre>
--- p 56-56
<p>You get:</p>

--- h2 65-65
<h2 id="callouts-pick-the-right-voice">Callouts: pick the right voice</h2>
--- p 67-67
<p>A callout lifts one piece of advice out of the text. Choose the kind by what happens if the reader skips it: NOTE for context, TIP for a shortcut, IMPORTANT for what they must know, WARNING for a risk, CAUTION for anything that can't be undone.</p>
--- div 69-70
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Station ids</p>
<p>Write station ids as <code>LH-</code> plus two digits: LH-04, never LH4 or lh-04. Search finds them all that way.</p>
</div>
--- div 72-73
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-tip">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Keep the units</p>
<p>Paste readings with their units, straight from the API. Nobody should have to guess whether 12.6 means °C.</p>
</div>
--- div 75-76
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-important">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Every number has a date</p>
<p>"Mean pH 8.02" means nothing on its own. "Mean pH 8.02 at LH-04, week 46" is a fact.</p>
</div>
--- div 78-79
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-warning">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Flagged readings</p>
<p>A flagged reading may be wrong. Quote it only with its flag, and say why the flag was raised.</p>
</div>
--- div 81-82
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-caution">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Deleting history</p>
<p>Removing a reading from the open dataset can't be undone for people who already downloaded it. Ask Imani first.</p>
</div>
--- h2 84-84
<h2 id="more-callout-kinds">More callout kinds</h2>
--- p 86-86
<p>For stories and side notes, these kinds fold away. Closed, they make a colored index; click a title to open one.</p>
--- details 88-89
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note markdown-alert-kind-info">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">How a station gets its name</summary>
<p>Named stations take the nearest charted landmark: Gannet Point, Kittiwake Shoal, Heron Flats. The rest keep their numbers until someone earns the right to name one.</p>
</details>
--- details 91-92
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-tip markdown-alert-kind-success">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">What "deployed" means</summary>
<p>A buoy counts as deployed when its first reading passes quality control, not when it hits the water.</p>
</details>
--- details 94-95
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-warning markdown-alert-kind-question">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">Why ten minutes between readings?</summary>
<p>Ten minutes is short enough to catch a turning tide or a passing storm, and a 40-second wake keeps the battery alive through a northern winter.</p>
</details>
--- details 97-98
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-caution markdown-alert-kind-failure">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">What we got wrong in Season 2</summary>
<p>We published raw salinity before the sensors were calibrated. Two weeks of LH-09 had to be reissued, with an apology.</p>
</details>
--- details 100-101
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-caution markdown-alert-kind-danger">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">Never on deck alone</summary>
<p>A buoy swap needs two people on deck and one at the wheel. No exceptions, not even in a flat calm.</p>
</details>
--- details 103-104
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-caution markdown-alert-kind-bug">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">Known issue: clock drift after a battery dip</summary>
<p>A buoy whose battery dips too low can restart with its clock a few seconds off. Ingest corrects it, but mention it if timestamps look odd.</p>
</details>
--- details 106-107
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note markdown-alert-kind-abstract">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">This guide in one paragraph</summary>
<p>One reader, one job. Short sections with clear headings. Real numbers with units and dates. Plain, warm words. Alt text on every picture and links that work.</p>
</details>
--- details 109-110
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note markdown-alert-kind-todo">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">Before you publish</summary>
<p>Check the spelling and every link, read the page once aloud, and ask one person who wasn't in the room to read it too.</p>
</details>
--- h2 112-112
<h2 id="containers">Containers</h2>
--- p 114-114
<p>When an aside runs longer than a callout, use a container: a titled block that can hold paragraphs, lists or even a table.</p>
--- div 116-118
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Containers hold longer asides</p>
<p>Open one with three colons and a kind, then close it with three colons on a line of their own. Everything in between is ordinary Markdown.</p>
</div>
--- div 120-122
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-tip">
<p class="markdown-alert-title">Which one to use</p>
<p>A callout for one piece of advice, a container for a sidebar, and a <code>&lt;details&gt;</code> block for things most readers can skip.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="words-we-use">Words we use</h2>
--- p 143-143
<p>The same thing always gets the same word. These five come up on almost every page.</p>
--- dl 145-159
<dl>
<dt>Station</dt>
<dd>A fixed point in the Sound, <code>LH-01</code> to <code>LH-12</code>. Buoys come and go; stations stay.</dd>
<dt>Reading</dt>
<dd>One set of measurements from one station at one moment: temperature, salinity, pH, dissolved oxygen and wave height.</dd>
<dt>QC flag</dt>
<dd>A mark our quality-control engine puts on a doubtful reading. Most flagged readings are published with their flag; the rest are removed after a person reviews them.</dd>
<dt>Deployment</dt>
<dd>One buoy at one station, from the day it goes in to the day it comes out.</dd>
<dt>Bulletin</dt>
<dd>The weekly summary for the public. Gull drafts it, and a person signs it off.</dd>
</dl>

<h2 id="front-matter">Front matter</h2>
--- p 162-162
<p>Start each page with a few facts about it between two <code>---</code> lines. PerMark shows them as a properties card, with tags as chips. Five fields is plenty:</p>
--- pre 164-172
<pre><code class="language-yaml">---
title: Season 3 deployment plan
owner: Hana Kowalski
status: in progress
updated: 2026-11-15
tags: [plan, season-3, operations]
---
</code></pre>
--- p 174-174
