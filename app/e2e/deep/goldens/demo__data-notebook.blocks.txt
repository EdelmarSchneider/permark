--- h2 47-47
<h2 dir="auto" id="temperature-against-oxygen">Temperature against oxygen</h2>
--- p 49-49
<p dir="auto">Cold water holds more dissolved oxygen (O<sub>2</sub>), so we expected the two to move in opposite directions. To measure how tightly, we took daily means for each station and computed Pearson's correlation coefficient:</p>
--- p 51-53
<p dir="auto"><span data-math-style="display">
r = \frac{\sum_i (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sqrt{\sum_i (x_i-\bar x)^2}\,\sqrt{\sum_i (y_i-\bar y)^2}}
</span></p>
--- p 55-55
<p dir="auto">With <span data-math-style="inline">x</span> the daily mean temperature and <span data-math-style="inline">y</span> the daily mean oxygen over 1,074 station-days, <span data-math-style="inline">r = -0.81</span> and R<sup>2</sup> = 0.66: temperature alone explains about two thirds of the variation. The fitted slope is −0.21 mg/L per °C, a little steeper than solubility alone predicts (about −0.17 mg/L per °C at these temperatures, see <a href="ocean-chemistry.md#oxygen-and-temperature">Oxygen and temperature</a>). The difference is biology: warm water also speeds up the bacteria that use oxygen.</p>
--- h2 57-57
<h2 dir="auto" id="the-code">The code</h2>
--- p 59-59
<p dir="auto">The whole analysis fits in a dozen lines of pandas. Lines 6 to 9 do the work: group by station and day, keep the full days, correlate, fit a straight line.</p>
--- pre 61-73
<pre><code class="language-python" data-lines="6-9" data-title="notebooks/do_vs_temp.py">import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_parquet("data/tidewell-90d.parquet")  # passed + flagged readings
df["day"] = df["time"].dt.date
g = df.groupby(["station", "day"])[["temp_c", "do_mgl"]]
daily = g.mean()[g.size() &gt;= 120].dropna()  # 120+ of 144 readings
r = daily["temp_c"].corr(daily["do_mgl"])  # Pearson is the default
slope, intercept = np.polyfit(daily["temp_c"], daily["do_mgl"], deg=1)

print(f"r = {r:.2f}  R² = {r * r:.2f}  slope = {slope:.2f} mg/L per °C")
</code></pre>

<h2 dir="auto" id="drift-at-kittiwake-shoal">Drift at Kittiwake Shoal</h2>
--- div 110-111
<div class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-caution markdown-alert-kind-bug">
<p class="markdown-alert-title" dir="auto">The pH at LH-07 is sliding</p>
<p dir="auto">In early November, pH at Kittiwake Shoal began to fall faster than the water around it<sup class="footnote-ref"><a data-footnote-ref="" href="#fn-__inline_1" id="fnref-__inline_1">1</a></sup>. On November 12 the trend reached −0.008 a day against the station's own 14-day median, 80 % of the drift limit, and the QC engine logged a warning (see the <a href="station/ingest-2026-11-12.log">ingest log</a>). Nothing is flagged yet: Beatriz will check the sensor against a water sample before winter maintenance starts on December 1.</p>
</div>

<h2 dir="auto" id="method-notes">Method notes</h2>
--- details 137-141
<details class="markdown-alert markdown-alert-note markdown-alert-kind-info">
<summary class="markdown-alert-title">Method notes</summary>
<ul>
<li dir="auto">Times are UTC. A daily mean needs at least 120 of the day's 144 readings; 6 of the 1,080 station-days fell short.</li>
<li dir="auto">Oxygen is measured optically as dissolved <span data-math-style="inline">\ce{O2}</span> and corrected for salinity before it reaches the API.</li>
<li dir="auto">Correlations use the open dataset only: readings that passed, plus flagged readings we kept.</li>
<li dir="auto">Weeks are ISO weeks. Week 46 runs November 9 to 15, so it has six days in this dataset.</li>
</ul>
</details>
