        p.pm-wide dir="auto"
          img src="/@permark-deep/project/demo/assets/wave-anatomy.svg" alt="Diagram of a wave over the still-water level: crest, trough, wavelength L from c…"
        p dir="auto"
          "For small waves, linear wave theory ties the angular frequency"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="\omega = 2\pi/T"
          "to the wavenumber"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="k = 2\pi/L"
          "and the water depth"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="h"
          ":"
        p dir="auto"
          span data-math-style="display"
            span.katex-display
              katex tex="\omega^2 = g k \tanh(kh) \tag{2}"
        p dir="auto"
          "In deep water ("
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="kh \gg 1"
          ", in practice deeper than half a wavelength),"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="\tanh(kh) \to 1"
          "and the period alone sets speed and length:"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="c = gT/2\pi"
          "and"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="L = gT^2/2\pi"
          ". An 8-second swell travels at"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="c \approx \pu{12.5 m/s}"
          "and measures"
          span data-math-style="inline"
            katex tex="L \approx \pu{99.9 m}"
          "from crest to crest. Over 20 m of water the same swell already feels the bottom: equation (2) gives about 89 m and 11.1 m/s."
